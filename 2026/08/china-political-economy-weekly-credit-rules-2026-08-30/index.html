China Political Economy Weekly | Aug 24–30, 2026: Credit Rules Are Being Rewritten

Posted on 2026-08-30 In Money & Markets Word count in article: 1.8k Reading time ≈ 6 mins.
Industrial profits are recovering unevenly as China redesigns property credit, manages liquidity without a new LPR signal, and brings AI into payment governance.

Weekly thesis China is not simply preparing a larger round of broad stimulus. This week it began rewriting how credit is controlled: industrial profits are improving unevenly, property finance is being reorganized around projects and delivery, the central bank is managing liquidity without sending a new LPR signal, and AI is moving into payment responsibility and computing infrastructure governance.

Reporting window: August 24–30, 2026 (Beijing time). Published August 30, 2026.

Languages: English · 中文 · 日本語

Executive Summary

  • Profit growth is still narrow. Industrial profits rose 17.6% in January–July, but electronics and resource-linked sectors carried much of the improvement while autos, building materials and utilities remained weak. Receivables and inventories continued to rise, so the headline does not yet describe a broad demand recovery.
  • Property policy is becoming a credit-infrastructure redesign. New rules combine project lead banks, longer development-loan maturities for current-sale projects, a 40-year ceiling for personal mortgages, later disbursement of presale mortgages and tighter project-account controls. Securities financing is also being organized around projects rather than unrestricted developer balance sheets.
  • Liquidity is being kept available while price signals remain restrained. The central bank rolled over 5tn yuan of one-year MLF against 6tn yuan of maturities and scheduled large overnight reverse repos. This looks more like targeted liquidity management than a direct promise of an LPR cut.
  • AI is entering the responsibility layer of finance. A self-regulatory convention for agent payments calls for “know your agent” checks, clear authorization, licensed institutions and testing, monitoring and rollback before autonomous payment.
  • Personnel handling is now part of the political-economy signal. Four military delegates lost their National People’s Congress qualifications; Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli also lost Central Military Commission posts. Public notices did not disclose reasons, so the report stays with verifiable institutional facts.

Industrial profits: aggregate improvement, narrow base

The National Bureau of Statistics reported that profits at large industrial firms reached RMB 4.582tn in January–July, up 17.6% year on year. Revenue rose 6.5% and the operating margin increased to 5.66%, up 0.54 percentage points.National Bureau of Statistics

This is better described as structural profit repair than as a synchronized domestic recovery. Electronics, non-ferrous metals, chemicals and mining may be benefiting from sector position, prices or export composition, while autos, construction materials and utilities remain under pressure. The combination of higher receivables and inventories is a reminder that accounting profit has not necessarily become healthy cash flow.

The useful follow-up questions are whether profits turn into cash, whether private firms receive new orders, whether inventories fall and whether price competition eases in traditional sectors.

Property policy is moving from borrower identity to projects and delivery

On August 28, the central bank and financial regulator released a 36-article property-credit opinion. The package includes:

  • a lead-bank system in which one bank manages the financing of a project;
  • development-loan maturities of up to five years for presale projects and up to seven years for current-sale projects;
  • a 40-year maximum for personal housing loans;
  • disbursement of presale mortgages only after completion filing; and
  • project-company or presale-supervision accounts for loan proceeds.Xinhua summary of the PBOC and financial-regulator opinion

The CSRC separately supported listed-developer refinancing, corporate bonds, CMBS, ABS, rental-housing and urban-renewal REITs, while emphasizing project-level financing and more penetrating disclosure controls.CSRC opinion

This is not a one-way property easing. It supports viable projects while trying to isolate presale and group-credit risks. For buyers, later mortgage disbursement and closed-loop funds may reduce delivery risk. For developers, however, the conversion of presales into usable cash may take longer and project-level execution becomes more important.

In the short run, some presale projects could see slower mortgage conversion. The longer-term test is whether projects can actually obtain a lead bank, whether supervised funds remain inside the project and whether current-sale and second-hand transactions improve. The rules do not prove that national home prices have bottomed.

Liquidity management is still ahead of broad monetary easing

On August 25, the PBOC conducted RMB 500bn of one-year MLF operations. With RMB 600bn maturing in the month, MLF was a net contraction of RMB 100bn. The central bank also scheduled overnight reverse repos from August 27 to September 1, with a daily ceiling of RMB 600bn.PBOC operation report

Caixin’s earlier explanation of the overnight tool stressed its role in both emergency and routine short-term liquidity management, rather than treating every operation as a new policy-rate signal.Caixin context

The weekly combination is best summarized as liquidity kept usable, price signals kept cautious. A smaller MLF rollover is not automatically tightening, and a large overnight facility is not automatically broad easing. An LPR decision still depends on policy rates, deposit costs, bank net interest margins and fiscal execution. There was no new LPR-cut signal this week.

AI and finance are entering the authorization and accountability layer

Issue No. 1220 of Caixin Weekly placed “AI infrastructure: Southeast Asia’s next battleground” on its cover, alongside pieces on personal-information protection, AI portfolio repositioning and L3 autonomous driving. The issue’s public table of contents frames AI competition as a combination of computing, energy, cross-border deployment, data governance and commercialization—not just model quality.Caixin Weekly No. 1220

The cover story’s public summary describes Southeast Asia as a recipient of global computing spillovers and notes the participation of Chinese data-center operators, colocation providers, and power, energy and liquid-cooling suppliers. The weekly broadcast identifies local-grid balancing as a major constraint.Cover-story summary Weekly broadcast My inference from these public summaries is that Chinese AI expansion abroad means exporting data-center, power and operations capabilities as well as models or servers. Policy monitoring therefore needs to track approvals, electricity and risk pricing across borders, not only domestic computing capacity.

On August 24, the China Payment Clearing Association released a self-regulatory convention for agent payments. It calls for “know your agent” checks, clear user authorization, identity and intent verification, licensed institutions for core payment and clearing functions, and reporting, evaluation, security testing, monitoring and rollback before an agent makes autonomous payments.Xinhua report

The National Data Administration said national intelligent-computing capacity had reached 2.45m PFLOPS (FP16) by the end of July, with the eight national computing hubs and three computing-power/electricity coordination zones accounting for more than 85% of the total.National Data Administration

Together, these developments move AI governance toward practical questions: who authorized a transaction, who executed it, who bears the loss, and how can the system be stopped? Those controls are becoming part of the product, not a legal appendix.

The political signal is implementation and accountability

On August 28, the Standing Committee of the National People’s Congress ended the representative qualifications of Zhang Youxia, Zhong Shaojun, Liu Zhenli and Ju Qiansheng. Zhang Youxia was removed as vice-chair of the Central Military Commission and Liu Zhenli as a CMC member.Caixin report

The public announcement confirms the institutional actions but does not explain the reasons. The safe analytical boundary is therefore to describe the personnel and qualification decisions, not to present undisclosed motives, factional interpretations or later criminal outcomes as facts.

Shanghai’s deputy party secretary Zhu Zhongming also appeared on August 29 as municipal-government party secretary. His background spans finance, audit and central economic agencies, but a formal appointment as mayor had not been confirmed by the cutoff.Caixin personnel report

External pressure remains a policy risk, not an implemented shock

On August 27, the Ministry of Commerce responded to the possibility that the United States could launch a Section 301 investigation into 16 economies over alleged “overcapacity” and potentially impose an additional 7.5% tariff on China. China opposed the move and reserved the right to take necessary measures.Xinhua summary of the Ministry of Commerce briefing

As of the cutoff, 7.5% was a possible measure, not an effective tariff. Its impact would depend on the product list, implementation timetable, exemptions and firms’ ability to pass through costs.

What to watch next

  • Official PMI new orders, construction activity and the small-enterprise index;
  • local implementation of property-credit, current-sale and presale-fund rules;
  • September open-market operations, policy rates and LPR quotations;
  • whether industrial profit growth becomes lower receivables, lower inventories and better private-sector cash flow;
  • the product scope and timetable of the U.S. Section 301 process;
  • the first reporting, testing or licensed pilots for agent payments; and
  • further confirmation of Shanghai’s mayoral appointment and military personnel decisions.

Further questions

  • How much of the new property-credit framework will become project-level funding and completed deliveries rather than merely revised bank procedures?
  • Will industrial profit growth broaden beyond electronics and resource-linked sectors, or remain concentrated in a few policy-supported industries?
  • Can agent-payment rules make authorization, liability and rollback auditable without slowing useful financial applications?
  • Will the absence of a new LPR signal persist if demand and private cash flow remain weak into September?
