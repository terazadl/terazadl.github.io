Japan Political Economy Weekly | Aug 10–16, 2026: Yen Nears 160 Under Takaichi’s Fiscal Pressure

Posted on 2026-08-17 In Japan , Money & Markets Word count in article: 1.2k Reading time ≈ 4 mins.
The yen returned toward 160 as upstream costs stayed high and Takaichi’s food-tax cut raised funding questions; Russia and Yasukuni added diplomatic risk.

Coverage: Aug 10–16, 2026 (Japan time).
Language versions: 中文 · English · 日本語

This week Coordinated intervention only temporarily supported the yen. Imported-cost pressure remained high, while Prime Minister Takaichi’s food-tax proposal exposed a roughly ¥5 trillion funding dispute. Putin’s Iturup visit and the August 15 memorial wording added diplomatic risk.

Executive summary

  • USD/JPY returned to the 159 area by Aug 14, recovering most of the post-intervention move. Yen weakness still reflects the US–Japan rate gap, fiscal expectations and uncertainty over BOJ timing.
  • The BOJ’s July domestic corporate-goods price index rose 7.2% y/y, just below June’s 7.3% and the 7.4% market expectation. Yen-denominated import prices remained around 30% higher y/y.
  • The government’s planned food consumption-tax cut (8% to 1% from April 2027 for two years) moved into a dispute over roughly ¥5 trillion of permanent funding and the central–local burden.
  • July Economy Watchers’ current DI rose 1.7 points to 45.7, its third monthly improvement, but stayed below the 50 expansion line.
  • President Putin visited Iturup (Etorofu), narrowing room for Japan–Russia rapprochement. Takaichi’s first August 15 address omitted the word “remorse”; four ministers visited Yasukuni, prompting Chinese and Korean protests.
  • The Nikkei rose for four straight sessions to 68,713.80 on Aug 14, while ASICS lifted full-year sales guidance to ¥1.05 trillion. Equity and brand strength should not be confused with better household purchasing power.

Core news

1. The yen returns toward 160

Confirmed | Aug 10–14

USD/JPY traded around 159.24–159.28 in Tokyo on Aug 14 and in the high 159s in New York. The market recovered the move caused by the July 31 coordinated intervention.

Why it matters (analysis): Intervention changed short-term trading risk but did not remove the rate differential, fiscal or BOJ-timing questions. A quick return to the old range would test policy credibility. Reuters Japan · Minkabu

2. Corporate-goods inflation remains 7.2%

Confirmed | Aug 13

The BOJ’s July domestic corporate-goods price index rose 7.2% y/y, versus 7.3% in June and 7.4% expected; it rose 0.1% m/m. Non-ferrous metals, oil and chemicals led the increase.

Why it matters (analysis): High upstream costs can squeeze smaller firms if they cannot pass them through, while further pass-through keeps pressure on households and the BOJ. BOJ CGPI · NLI Research

3. Food-tax cut enters a ¥5 trillion funding dispute

Policy direction confirmed; funding details media-reported | Aug 10

The government is targeting April 2027 to reduce the food consumption-tax rate from 8% to 1% for two years. The latest debate concerns whether about ¥5 trillion can be found through spending and revenue measures, and how any linked transfers would be shared with local governments.

Why it matters (analysis): A tax cut can support consumption, but optimistic revenue assumptions, fund reallocations or future spending cuts could weaken bond-market confidence. Background reporting

4. Economy Watchers improve for a third month, still below 50

Confirmed | Aug 10

The July current-conditions DI rose 1.7 points to 45.7; the outlook DI rose 0.1 to 45.8. Household, corporate and employment readings all improved.

Why it matters (analysis): Sentiment is no longer deteriorating, but a sub-50 reading is not a domestic-demand recovery. Cabinet Office · NLI Research

5. Putin’s first Iturup visit narrows diplomatic room

Confirmed | Aug 13

Putin visited Iturup (Etorofu), controlled by Russia and claimed by Japan. Takaichi protested, Japan summoned Russia’s ambassador and Moscow rejected the protest.

Why it matters (analysis): The peace-treaty and territorial track is effectively more frozen, narrowing Japan’s room on energy, sanctions and northern security. Prime Minister’s Office · AP

6. August 15 wording and Yasukuni visits raise China–Korea friction

Confirmed | Aug 15

Takaichi’s memorial address retained a pledge not to repeat the tragedy of war but omitted “remorse” and “pledge of non-war” used in the prior year. She did not personally visit Yasukuni; four ministers did, drawing protests from China and South Korea.

Why it matters (analysis): The combination signals restraint at the prime-ministerial level and continuity for domestic conservatives. Historical-risk premia in regional diplomacy rise even if practical cooperation continues. Address · Jiji

7. The Nikkei rises four sessions, but households do not automatically benefit

Confirmed | Aug 14

The Nikkei closed at 68,713.80, up 405.21 points, with a weekly gain of more than 3,000 points. Lower US inflation, technology shares, a weak yen and earnings upgrades supported risk appetite.

Why it matters (analysis): The index trades global liquidity, AI and yen translation effects. Food, energy and import prices still determine household welfare. Market review · LIMO

8. ASICS lifts full-year sales guidance to ¥1.05 trillion

Confirmed | Aug 14

First-half sales rose 32.7% to ¥534.4 billion and operating profit 48.5% to ¥120.4 billion. Full-year sales guidance rose from ¥950 billion to ¥1.05 trillion.

Why it matters (analysis): ASICS shows the difference between genuine global brand pricing power and a simple yen translation gain. ASICS IR · TV Asahi

Politics and institutions

There was no new, methodologically comparable approval poll meeting the reporting standard this week. The food-tax plan moved from “whether” to “who pays and whether funding is permanent.” The Iturup visit and August 15 wording increase diplomatic friction with Russia, China and South Korea. The Diet’s autumn session remains the institutional test for tax legislation.

Policy and market implications (analysis)

  1. BOJ: High corporate-goods inflation and a weak yen keep hikes possible, but below-50 sentiment argues for a gradual pace.
  2. Households: The food-tax cut is not scheduled until April 2027; near-term purchasing power depends on wages, energy support and the yen.
  3. Yen and JGBs: If ¥5 trillion of permanent funding is not credible, fiscal expansion could offset the currency benefit of intervention or future hikes and lift term premium.
  4. Equities: Global technology and yen translation can keep the Nikkei strong while the household economy remains under pressure.
  5. Industry: Global brands with pricing power are structurally different from exporters that only benefit from a weak yen.
