## Executive summary

- Real GDP rose **0.3% q/q**, or **1.1% annualised**, but domestic demand subtracted **0.2 percentage points** and net exports added 0.5 points. Consumption fell 0.1% q/q and equipment investment 1.2%.
- July core CPI rose **1.8% y/y** (1.6% in June); CPI excluding fresh food and energy rose 1.9%. Food inflation was 3.0%.
- The 10-year JGB yield briefly reached **2.945%** on Aug 18, the highest since 1996. Reports suggested a possible **3.8%** FY2027 budget-rate assumption; this was not yet official during the week.
- July imports grew faster than exports, producing a **¥634.5 billion** trade deficit. Alternative crude routes restored physical supply but increased freight and energy costs.
- Core machinery orders rebounded **9.7% m/m**, but one large non-ferrous-metals order exaggerated the headline.
- Kyodo’s Aug 22–23 telephone poll put Cabinet approval at **50.2%**, its lowest since the Takaichi Cabinet formed. Opposition-party merger talks remained stuck.
- Rakuten was reported to be helping German defence start-up Helsing market the HX-2 AI strike drone to the Ground Self-Defense Force; testing and procurement were not officially confirmed.

## Core news

### 1. Q2 GDP grew 1.1% annualised, while domestic demand contracted

**Confirmed | Aug 17**

Real GDP increased 0.3% q/q and nominal GDP 1.2%. Domestic demand contributed **-0.2 points**; net exports contributed **+0.5 points**. Household consumption fell 0.1%, housing 0.5% and equipment investment 1.2%; exports rose 0.5% and imports fell 1.5%.

**Why it matters (analysis):** Lower imports mechanically lift net exports in GDP accounting. The headline therefore overstates the strength of the household and corporate economy. [Cabinet Office GDP](https://www.esri.cao.go.jp/jp/sna/data/data_list/sokuhou/gaiyou/pdf/main_1.pdf) · [NRI analysis](https://www.nri.com/jp/media/column/kiuchi/20260817.html)

### 2. Ten-year JGB yields approach 3%; a 3.8% budget assumption is reported

**Market data confirmed; budget assumption was still media-reported | Aug 18–21**

The 10-year yield touched **2.945%** on Aug 18 and the new issue closed at 2.870% on Aug 21. Reports said the MOF was considering a 3.8% rate for FY2027 interest-cost estimates, versus 3.0% in the FY2026 budget.

**Why it matters (analysis):** Existing debt is not instantly repriced at 3.8%, but refinancing costs rise over time. Tax cuts and expansionary spending will face a stronger bond-market test. [Reuters/Investing](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/japans-10year-government-bond-yield-rises-to-threedecade-peak-4864126) · [JGB rates](https://www.bb.jbts.co.jp/ja/historical/main_rate.htm)

### 3. July core CPI returns to 1.8%, with food prices still high

**Confirmed | Aug 21**

Headline CPI rose 1.9% y/y, core CPI 1.8% and core-core CPI 1.9%. Fresh-food-excluding food prices rose 3.0%; fuel-tax and related measures reduced headline CPI by an estimated 0.35 points.

**Why it matters (analysis):** Subsidised gasoline lowers the headline while food and services keep rising. This preserves a case for BOJ normalisation but argues for a careful pace while demand is weak. [Statistics Bureau CPI](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/cpi/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/zenkoku.pdf)

### 4. July trade deficit reaches ¥634.5 billion

**Confirmed | Aug 18 and 20**

Exports rose 23.2% y/y to ¥11.5118 trillion and imports 27.8% to ¥12.1463 trillion. Export volume rose 5.2%, import volume 1.2%, while the customs exchange rate was ¥161.83 per dollar. Cars and semiconductor parts lifted exports; crude, semiconductor inputs and non-ferrous metals lifted imports.

**Why it matters (analysis):** Japan’s energy problem is shifting from availability to delivered cost. A weak yen, oil prices and longer shipping routes can worsen the terms of trade even when physical volumes recover. [MOF trade statistics](https://www.customs.go.jp/toukei/shinbun/trade-st/gaiyo2026_07.pdf) · [Daiwa](https://www.dir.co.jp/report/research/economics/japan/20260820_025996.html)

### 5. Machinery orders rebound 9.7%, but the signal is narrow

**Confirmed | Aug 19**

Core private machinery orders rose 9.7% m/m; manufacturing orders rose 19.9% and non-manufacturing 4.5%. Q2 core orders rose only 0.2%, and the survey expected 4.9% growth in Q3. A single large non-ferrous-metals order lifted the monthly result.

**Why it matters (analysis):** Capital spending has not collapsed, but one month cannot prove a broad investment recovery. [Cabinet Office machinery orders](https://www.esri.cao.go.jp/jp/stat/juchu/2026/2606gaiyou.pdf)

### 6. Kyodo approval poll falls to 50.2%

**Media poll | Aug 22–23**

Kyodo’s nationwide telephone survey put approval at **50.2%**, down 3.5 points, and disapproval at 33.9%. Different pollsters and methods should not be blended.

**Why it matters (analysis):** The Cabinet still has a workable base, but disaster response and tax-cut promises have not produced stable political dividends. [Kyodo via livedoor](https://news.livedoor.com/article/detail/32137534/)

### 7. Opposition merger talks remain stuck

**Media reports | Aug 18 and 21**

The Constitutional Democratic Party, Komeito and Chudo Reform Alliance aimed for an end-August conclusion, but local organisations and policy differences remained obstacles.

**Why it matters (analysis):** A failed merger keeps opposition votes fragmented; a forced merger could create new organisational costs. [TV Asahi](https://news.tv-asahi.co.jp/news_politics/articles/000527106.html) · [FNN](https://www.fnn.jp/articles/-/1098782)

### 8. Rakuten reportedly markets Helsing’s AI strike drone

**Media report; testing and procurement not officially confirmed | Aug 17–18**

Rakuten was reported to be helping German defence company Helsing promote the HX-2 to the GSDF. Tests were said to run through September, but the Defence Ministry had not tied the system to a confirmed procurement programme.

**Why it matters (analysis):** Japan’s defence supply chain may combine traditional primes with software and AI start-ups. Compliance, testing and procurement cycles remain the bottlenecks. [AeroTime](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/rakuten-helsing-hx-2-strike-drone-japan)

## Politics and institutions

Kyodo’s 50.2% reading should be tracked only within Kyodo’s own series. The reported 3.8% budget rate is an interest-cost assumption, not a guaranteed market yield. No parliamentary vote this week changed the path of food-tax reform; the autumn Diet and opposition coordination remain the key institutional tests.

## Policy and market implications (analysis)

1. **BOJ:** Core inflation and a weak yen keep normalisation possible, but weak consumption and investment argue against aggressive hikes.
2. **Fiscal policy:** A higher budget-rate assumption raises the opportunity cost of unfunded tax cuts and puts term premium in focus.
3. **Households:** Positive GDP does not guarantee better living standards when food inflation, energy bills and poor terms of trade erode real income.
4. **Yen and equities:** Yen weakness supports exporters but raises import costs; a rising Nikkei is not evidence of stronger household purchasing power.
5. **Industry:** Energy procurement, defence software and labour-saving investment may grow, but logistics and compliance costs determine who captures the margin.
