## Executive summary

- **Household spending slumps:** Two-or-more-person households cut real spending by 3.6% y/y in July, marking eight consecutive months in negative territory and reflecting defensive consumer retrenchment.
- **Debt-servicing budget hits record:** The Ministry of Finance aggregated FY2027 general-account budget requests at **¥117.5 trillion**, with debt-servicing costs climbing near **¥30 trillion** under an elevated interest-rate assumption of 2.1%+.
- **Real wages stall near zero:** July nominal cash earnings grew 3.6%, but inflation-adjusted real wages barely hovered at **+0.4% y/y**, leaving household purchasing power vulnerable to sticky food and utility bills.
- **Retail JGB yields step up:** MOF raised the initial coupon for 10-year variable retail JGBs to **0.72%** (the highest since 2012), accelerating the repricing of domestic funding costs and floating mortgages.
- **September rate-hike pricing holds firm:** Driven by Middle East energy risks and JGB 10-year yields touching 1.06%, swap markets maintained high probabilities for an eventual policy rate hike to **1.25%**.
- **Semiconductor supply chain friction:** China’s MOFCOM imposed provisional anti-dumping security deposits on Japanese dichlorosilane, injecting trade friction into high-purity precursor gas exports.

## Core developments

### 1. Real household spending contracts 3.6% y/y in July
MIC's Family Income and Expenditure Survey showed average household consumption fell 3.6% in real terms (nominal +0.1%). Outlays on food (-1.7%), transport/telecom (-4.2%), and housing (-6.8%) all declined. Despite spring wage agreements, households are aggressively trimming discretionary spending under chronic import-led inflation.

### 2. FY2027 budget requests surge to ¥117.5T on debt-servicing costs
Central-government requests hit a fourth consecutive all-time high. With the benchmark calculation yield raised above 2.1%, annual debt servicing is approaching ¥30 trillion. Every 100 bp increase in long-term rates will progressively crowd out fiscal flexibility across social security and industrial policy.

### 3. Real wages hover at +0.4% as inflation eats nominal gains
MHLW's preliminary Monthly Labour Survey recorded nominal wage growth of 3.6% y/y (31st month of expansion), but persistent CPI inflation eroded nearly all nominal improvements, keeping real wage growth essentially flat.

### 4. Retail JGB coupons raised to 0.72%
The Finance Ministry's new 10-year variable retail bond tranche offered 0.72%, reflecting rising benchmark yields. While boosting interest income for elderly savers, it signals higher deposit-gathering costs for major banks and upward pressure on mortgage lending rates.

### 5. Hawkish expectations test the BOJ ahead of September 17–18
With 10-year JGB yields consolidating at 1.06% and oil prices supported by Middle East tensions, the BOJ faces an acute policy trilemma: holding rates invites further yen depreciation and imported inflation, while hiking risks compounding household mortgage distress and government debt charges.

### 6. Chinese anti-dumping measures on Japanese dichlorosilane
China’s Ministry of Commerce initiated provisional anti-dumping deposits on imports of Japanese dichlorosilane, a critical precursor gas for epitaxial silicon and advanced wafer fabrication. This escalates bilateral tech friction into upstream chemical materials.

## Structural judgment

> Japan's fundamental vulnerability is not the nominal exchange rate level, but the growing misalignment between policy rate normalization and domestic absorption capacity. With household consumption shrinking for eight straight months, the policy triangle—price stability, domestic demand, and fiscal debt service—now operates with virtually zero safety margin.
